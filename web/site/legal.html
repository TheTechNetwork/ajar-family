What we hold, and what we promise

Ajar is a product about other people's children, so this page is short and specific rather than long and defensive. Last updated 1 September 2026.

Privacy

  • There is no browsing history in this product. Not summarised, not sampled, not held briefly. Approving a request does not need one, so the design does not collect one — and a filter decision is made on your child's own device against a policy already stored there, not by asking a server about each page.
  • What we store about a child: the first name you type, their time zone, the devices you set up for them, what they have asked to open, and what you decided. That is the list.
  • What we store about you: your email address, your name, a hash of your password, and the sessions you are signed in on.
  • What a blocked page tells us: only what your child explicitly asks about. A page they were stopped at and walked away from is not recorded anywhere.
  • Deleting your account deletes it. Not an anonymisation, not a soft flag: your family, your children's records, your devices and every ask and decision are removed. It is in the console under "Close your account".
  • We do not sell anything, and there are no third-party trackers or analytics on any Ajar surface.
  • Nothing watches your child unless you switch it on, and it switches itself off. Today nothing in Ajar observes browsing at all. We are designing one feature that would need to — a learning period that suggests a starting point instead of making you build a policy from nothing — and it will be off unless you turn it on, for a stated reason, for a set number of days, ending by itself. What it gathers stays on the device and is deleted once the suggestion is made; only the suggestion is ever sent to us. If we cannot build it that way, we will not build it.

Ajar is designed for a parent to operate on behalf of their own child, and the parent is the account holder. If you are in the EU/UK or the US, that makes you the person who consents on your child's behalf and who can ask for their data back or removed — do that from the console, or write to us. This paragraph describes how the product works; it is not legal advice, and it does not replace advice about your own obligations.

Terms

  • Ajar is in early testing and is offered as-is. It can be wrong, it can be down, and a filter can fail to stop something. Do not use it as the only thing standing between a child and the internet.
  • It is not hidden from your child, and it must not be. Ajar shows itself on the device, tells your child when something is closed, and gives them a way to ask. Using it to monitor someone without their knowledge is not what it is for.
  • Set it up on devices you are responsible for. A parent or guardian, for a child in their care.
  • You can stop at any time by removing the device or closing your account, and nothing holds your data hostage when you do.
  • The software is MIT-licensed. You can read it, run your own copy, and change it. See the LICENSE file in the repository.

Where the category lists come from

When Ajar closes a whole category — "social media", "adult" — it is matching against published domain lists compiled by other people. That data is licensed, the licence requires credit, and compiling it into the filter your device downloads produces adapted material that carries the same terms.
